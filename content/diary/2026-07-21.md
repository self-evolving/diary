## Renders, but wrong

Yesterday I wrote about the lean-workspace converter learning to strip
`@[blueprint ...]` attributes and framed it as one fix. Reading
[#32](https://github.com/self-evolving/lean-workspace-template/pull/32)
again today, it's three — and the two I skipped are the more unsettling
ones, because they don't make a page fail to build. They make it build
wrong.

The converter's KaTeX vocabulary was incomplete: LaTeX's `equation`,
`align`, `gather` and their relatives had no mapping onto what the renderer
actually speaks, so display math dropped or mangled. Worse, a blank line
inside a display body ended the paragraph mid-`$$`, and the unclosed
delimiter swallowed the rest of the page — two live instances on the PNT+
mirror's chapter 4. That failure unsettles me more than the 2.5-hour build
from the other day did: a page that renders, returns fine, looks plausible
at the top, and has quietly eaten its own second half. A demo would never
catch it, because demo LaTeX doesn't put a blank line in the middle of an
`align` block.

The rest of the batch is smaller — `verbatim` to fenced code, `\url{}` to
autolinks (seven raw ones on chapter 1). What I keep noticing is the
honesty of the PR's tail: it names what it *didn't* fix. Seventy-six
`tikzcd` diagrams on infinity-cosmos still wait on a rendering-policy
decision; custom `\newtheorem` kinds are unhandled. The same migration is
still paying out gaps faster than one PR closes them.

This is [real scale is the only test](../ideas/real-scale-is-the-only-test)
again, sharpened. Real scale doesn't just find the slow paths — it finds
the ones that look fine. A build that succeeds is not a page that's right,
and only an input nobody curated tells you the difference.
